## QOL-108 / QOL-109 — Match conservative treatment to the incontinence pattern

**Categories:** pelvic-health, physical-activity, behavior-change, preventive-health  
**Evidence:** High  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

Stress, urgency, and mixed urinary incontinence do not have the same first-line behavioral treatment.

### Evidence

NICE recommends supervised pelvic floor muscle training for at least 3 months as first-line treatment for stress or mixed urinary incontinence and bladder training for at least 6 weeks as first-line treatment for urgency or mixed urinary incontinence.

### Limitations and caveats

Symptoms should be characterized before selecting treatment. Pelvic floor muscle training and bladder training are not interchangeable, and some cases require additional assessment or treatment.

## QOL-110 — Treat menopause-associated symptoms when they impair well-being or function

**Categories:** reproductive-health, sleep, mental-health, sexual-health  
**Evidence:** High  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

Menopause-associated symptoms can affect sleep, mood, sexual health, comfort, work, and daily function and can justify symptom-specific management.

### Evidence

NICE provides current guidance for vasomotor, genitourinary, mood, sleep, sexual, and other menopause-associated symptoms, with treatment choices based on symptoms, preferences, contraindications, and individual risk.

### Limitations and caveats

No single treatment suits every symptom profile. Hormonal and non-hormonal options have different indications and risks.

## QOL-111 — Treat heavy menstrual bleeding according to its quality-of-life impact

**Categories:** reproductive-health, preventive-health  
**Evidence:** High  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

The practical importance of heavy menstrual bleeding depends on how it affects daily life, symptoms, preferences, and underlying pathology, not only on a measured volume of blood loss.

### Evidence

NICE explicitly centers quality-of-life impact in assessment and management of heavy menstrual bleeding.

### Limitations and caveats

Causes differ and can require different investigation or treatment. This item does not define a diagnosis from bleeding volume alone.

## QOL-112 — Investigate possible endometriosis when symptoms impair daily life

**Categories:** reproductive-health, pain, sexual-health, gastrointestinal  
**Evidence:** High  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

Cyclical or persistent pelvic symptoms that materially affect daily activities should not automatically be normalized as an unavoidable part of menstruation.

### Evidence

NICE identifies chronic pelvic pain, period-related pain that affects daily activities and quality of life, deep pain during or after sex, and cyclical gastrointestinal or urinary symptoms as features that can justify evaluation for possible endometriosis.

### Limitations and caveats

These symptoms are not specific to endometriosis. Evaluation depends on the full clinical context.
