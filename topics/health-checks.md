## QOL-049 — Correct meaningful refractive error and reassess vision when symptomatic


### Idea

Visual blur, eye strain, or functional difficulty can justify a current eye examination even when the person previously had a correction or procedure.

### Why it may affect quality of life

Vision is used continuously for work, mobility, reading, communication, and leisure. A small but persistent deficit can create repeated effort or functional limitation.

### Evidence

A randomized trial in older adults found that correcting uncorrected refractive error improved vision-specific quality of life.

### Limitations and caveats

The trial population was older adults, so effect size cannot be assumed in younger groups. Eye fatigue can also arise from dry eye, prolonged near work, ocular disease, or other causes.

- [REF-023](../references/REF-023.md)

## QOL-050 / QOL-051 — Treat sensory or oral deficits when they affect function


### Idea

Painful oral disease and functional hearing difficulty can affect daily life far beyond the affected organ.

### Evidence

WHO identifies oral disease as a source of pain and functional impairment and identifies untreated hearing loss as a barrier to communication, employment, participation, and well-being.

### Practical applications

Seek appropriate dental or hearing evaluation when symptoms, pain, speech understanding, or daily function justify it.

### Limitations and caveats

Routine screening intervals and treatment choices depend on age, risk, local guidance, and symptoms.

- [REF-024](../references/REF-024.md)
- [REF-025](../references/REF-025.md)

## QOL-070 / QOL-071 / QOL-073 / QOL-074 — Sleep can be disrupted by a condition outside the sleep system


### Idea

Restless legs, nasal obstruction, symptomatic bruxism, and nocturnal reflux can present partly as poor sleep or poor daytime function. Treat the underlying condition when it is actually present.

### Evidence

AASM provides evidence-based treatment guidance for restless legs syndrome. A rhinitis RCT found that treatment improved congestion and some sleep/daytime outcomes. NIDCR describes clinically significant bruxism complications. NIDDK and randomized-trial synthesis support treating symptomatic GERD, including nocturnal presentations.

### Limitations and caveats

Each condition has its own diagnostic criteria. This map does not imply that nonspecific tiredness is evidence for any of them.

## QOL-072 — Treat recurrent headache or migraine when it impairs function


### Idea

Frequent or disabling headache should not be dismissed as an unavoidable background symptom.

### Evidence

WHO identifies headache disorders as major causes of pain, disability, lost productivity, and impaired quality of life. Preventive migraine treatment trials demonstrate that clinically meaningful improvements in headache impact and migraine-specific quality of life are possible in appropriate patients.

### Limitations and caveats

Headache type matters. New, sudden, severe, or neurologically unusual headache can require urgent medical assessment.

### References

- [REF-060](../references/REF-060.md)
- [REF-061](../references/REF-061.md)

## QOL-075 — Address digital eye strain when screen work causes symptoms


### Idea

Treat screen-related eye fatigue as a modifiable symptom when it appears consistently during prolonged near work.

### Evidence

A controlled crossover experiment found that break schedules reduced some digital-eye-strain symptoms during prolonged near work.

### Practical applications

Use visual breaks and reassess vision or ocular health if symptoms remain frequent.

### Limitations and caveats

The study was small. Persistent or unilateral visual symptoms, pain, or visual loss need appropriate assessment rather than a break schedule alone.

### References

- [REF-040](../references/REF-040.md)

## QOL-093 — Treat bothersome tinnitus rather than assuming nothing can help


### Idea

The realistic treatment target for tinnitus may be reduced distress and functional burden rather than elimination of the sound itself.

### Evidence

The VA/DoD tinnitus guideline summarizes evidence-based management options for bothersome tinnitus.

### Limitations and caveats

New unilateral tinnitus, pulsatile tinnitus, sudden hearing changes, or other concerning features can require specific medical evaluation.

### References

- [REF-047](../references/REF-047.md)

## QOL-094 — Distinguish waking to urinate from urinating because one is already awake


### Idea

Use the symptom definition carefully before deciding the bladder is the cause of fragmented sleep.

### Evidence

The International Continence Society defines nocturia around waking during the main sleep period to pass urine and recommends quantifying episodes.

### Limitations and caveats

Nocturia has multiple possible causes. A person who wakes for another reason and then chooses to urinate may have a different sleep problem.

### References

- [REF-065](../references/REF-065.md)

## QOL-095 — Investigate chronic GI symptoms that meaningfully affect QoL


### Idea

Recurring bowel symptoms deserve a symptom-based assessment when they materially affect daily life rather than automatic assignment to a popular diagnosis or food intolerance.

### Evidence

NIDDK guidance distinguishes diagnostic patterns such as IBS and explains that constipation can include hard, dry, or difficult-to-pass stools even when bowel movements occur.

### Practical applications

Characterize symptoms, duration, stool pattern, triggers, medications, diet, and red flags before selecting an intervention.

### Limitations and caveats

Blood in stool, unexplained weight loss, persistent severe pain, fever, vomiting, or other concerning features can change the urgency and differential diagnosis.

### References

- [REF-048](../references/REF-048.md)
- [REF-050](../references/REF-050.md)

## QOL-096 — Treat eczema/pruritus when it disrupts comfort or sleep


### Idea

Chronic itch and dermatitis can be a sleep and quality-of-life problem, not only a skin appearance problem.

### Evidence

A review of the atopic-dermatitis literature describes high rates of sleep disturbance, with pruritus and scratching among important mechanisms.

### Limitations and caveats

The reference is secondary evidence and much of the literature measures sleep as a secondary outcome.

### References

- [REF-070](../references/REF-070.md)

## QOL-097 — Review medications when symptom timing makes the link plausible


### Idea

When a new symptom begins after a medication is started or its dose changes, include adverse effects in the differential rather than assuming the symptom is a new personal baseline.

### Evidence

FDA guidance emphasizes that medicines can cause adverse reactions and provides official labeling and safety resources for evaluating them.

### Practical applications

Review the medication timeline and discuss plausible adverse effects with the prescriber or pharmacist.

### Limitations and caveats

Do not stop prescribed medicines abruptly on the basis of this item. Symptoms can have multiple causes and withdrawal can itself be harmful.

## QOL-114 — Treat cataract according to functional impact


Cataract surgery can be considered when cataract-related vision loss interferes with reading, driving, or other everyday activities. The decision depends on functional impact and clinical context rather than the mere presence of a cataract.

### References

- [REF-084](../references/REF-084.md)

## QOL-115 / QOL-116 — Rehabilitate hearing loss and prevent avoidable noise injury


Meaningful hearing loss can justify rehabilitation to reduce communication and participation barriers. Hazardous occupational noise exposure warrants source control where possible and hearing protection when indicated.

### Limitations and caveats

Hearing protection is one layer of a prevention program and should not replace feasible engineering or administrative noise controls.
