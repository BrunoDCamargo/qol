## QOL-059 — Use evidence-based psychological treatment for clinically significant anxiety

**Categories:** mental-health, preventive-health  
**Evidence:** High  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

When anxiety is persistent and functionally impairing, treat it as a clinical problem with evidence-based interventions rather than relying only on informal self-help.

### Why it may affect quality of life

Clinically significant anxiety can consume attention, drive avoidance, disturb sleep, and restrict work, social, and daily functioning.

### Evidence

WHO's 2023 mhGAP guideline gives a strong recommendation, with moderate certainty, for brief structured psychological interventions based on CBT principles for adults with generalized anxiety disorder or panic disorder.

### Practical applications

Use an appropriate evidence-based psychological treatment matched to the specific anxiety presentation and access context.

### Limitations and caveats

The cited WHO recommendation directly addresses GAD and panic disorder. Other anxiety disorders can require different protocols, and diagnosis should not be inferred from ordinary situational stress.

## QOL-060 — Address persistent rumination when it consumes attention or recovery

**Categories:** mental-health, attention, work-design  
**Evidence:** Low  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

Treat repetitive thinking as a target when it repeatedly extends a stressor beyond the event itself or prevents psychological recovery.

### Why it may affect quality of life

Rumination can keep attention attached to unresolved or uncontrollable problems and interfere with recovery periods.

### Evidence

WHO supports structured psychological approaches for clinically significant anxiety, and a randomized occupational intervention found cognitive-behavioral strategies improved psychological detachment from work. Neither source proves a single generic anti-rumination protocol works for all forms of repetitive thinking.

### Practical applications

A therapist may use cognitive, behavioral, metacognitive, exposure-based, or acceptance-oriented methods depending on the mechanism maintaining the rumination.

### Limitations and caveats

Rumination occurs across several diagnoses and also in normal stress. This item intentionally has a low evidence rating because its wording is broader than the cited interventions.

## QOL-061 — Treat meditation/mindfulness as a conditional tool

**Categories:** mental-health, attention  
**Evidence:** Low  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

Mindfulness can be useful in some contexts, but it should not displace a treatment with stronger evidence for a specific problem.

### Evidence

WHO includes mindfulness/relaxation among stress-management techniques that may be considered for GAD or panic disorder, with low certainty. A small pilot RCT with a structurally matched control did not find a clear mindfulness-specific stress benefit.

### Practical applications

Use mindfulness because the person finds it useful or because it fits an evidence-based program, not because it is assumed to be a universal mental-health intervention.

### Limitations and caveats

Programs vary substantially in dose, quality, population, and outcome.

## QOL-077 / QOL-092 — Treat low activation or depression as a real target

**Categories:** mental-health, behavior-change, preventive-health  
**Evidence:** Moderate  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

Persistent loss of interest, pleasure, or activity can warrant assessment and structured treatment. Behavioral activation is one evidence-based approach that deliberately reconnects behavior with meaningful or rewarding activities.

### Evidence

A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis synthesized 105 behavioral-activation trials for adult depression and found a substantial evidence base, while outcomes varied by comparison and study design.

### Practical applications

When low activation is part of depression or avoidance, scheduling specific meaningful activities can be a treatment component rather than waiting for motivation to return first.

### Limitations and caveats

Scheduling pleasant activities by itself is not equivalent to a full depression assessment or structured behavioral-activation treatment.

## QOL-081 / QOL-082 — Recovery from work is a modifiable process

**Categories:** work-design, mental-health, time  
**Evidence:** Moderate for detachment; Low for broad recovery training  
**Applicability:** Conditional

### Idea

When work continues psychologically into non-work time, interventions can target detachment and recovery rather than assuming time off automatically produces recovery.

### Evidence

A randomized trial with 393 workers found cognitive-behavioral and mindfulness-based interventions improved detachment. A smaller pilot RCT found broad improvements after recovery training, but that evidence needs replication.

### Practical applications

Use explicit end-of-work boundaries, cognitive strategies, transition routines, or structured recovery training when inability to disengage is a persistent problem.

### Limitations and caveats

If overload, instability, harassment, or another structural problem is the main driver, individual recovery techniques cannot substitute for changing the work condition.
