# Selection guide: Portable HEPA air purifier

Parent Implementation Option: [`IMP-017`](../../implementation-options/IMP-017.md)  
Parent QoL Item: [`QOL-047`](../../items/QOL-047.md)  
Last reviewed: 2026-09-14

## Decision this guide supports

Use this guide after deciding that portable particulate filtration is relevant under `QOL-047`. It helps compare concrete portable HEPA air purifiers. It does not determine whether filtration is needed in the first place and does not replace source control or ventilation.

## Context modifiers

Before comparing products, define the context that changes what good performance means:

- **Room area and ceiling height.** A purifier must deliver enough clean air for the space it serves. Higher ceilings increase the required capacity.
- **Target pollutant.** CADR is a particle-removal metric. Gas and VOC removal require different media and are not captured by particle CADR.
- **Noise tolerance.** A unit that is effective only at a fan speed the user will not tolerate may underperform in practice.
- **Expected run time.** Filtration depends on airflow and operating time. A quiet setting that can run continuously may be more useful than a high-speed mode used rarely.
- **Wildfire or unusually high particle load.** More aggressive sizing may be appropriate than under ordinary conditions.
- **Placement constraints.** The unit needs unobstructed airflow and enough space to operate where people spend time.

## Must-have criteria

### 1. Capacity matched to the room

Use independently verified Clean Air Delivery Rate where possible. EPA advises choosing a particle air cleaner with CADR large enough for the room. AHAM's consumer guidance uses smoke CADR of at least two-thirds of room area in square feet under ordinary conditions, with larger sizing for higher ceilings and more demanding conditions.

Treat manufacturer room-size claims cautiously when the underlying CADR or test method is unclear.

### 2. Credible particle-filtration performance

Prefer performance supported by an independent certification program or a transparent repeatable test method. AHAM Verifide publishes independently verified CADR values for certified models. Independent laboratories such as RTINGS can add information about how performance changes with operating mode and noise.

The presence of a HEPA label alone does not describe how much clean air the complete device delivers into the room.

### 3. Replacement filters that are realistically obtainable

Confirm filter model, replacement interval, price, and availability before buying. EPA notes that overloaded filters lose effectiveness. A purifier with unavailable or prohibitively expensive consumables can become unusable even if initial performance is good.

### 4. No intentional ozone generation

Exclude air cleaners designed to intentionally produce ozone for occupied spaces. EPA identifies ozone as a lung irritant and advises avoiding devices that intentionally generate it.

## Performance criteria

### Particle filtration

Compare verified smoke, dust, and pollen CADR when available. For fine-particle concerns, smoke CADR is the more relevant of the standard AHAM particle categories because it represents the smallest particles in that rating system.

### Performance at tolerable noise

Maximum-speed CADR is useful but incomplete. EPA notes that lower fan speeds filter less air, while noise can prevent people from running a purifier at effective settings. RTINGS added a low-noise particle-filtration test to its air-purifier methodology in 2026 because maximum-speed testing did not reflect common use well enough.

For bedrooms or quiet workspaces, give substantial weight to filtration performance at a noise level the user will actually tolerate.

### Gas and odor removal

Do not infer gas removal from HEPA performance or particle CADR. EPA states that gas removal requires activated carbon or another suitable sorbent and that there is no widely used performance rating system equivalent to CADR for residential gas removal.

### Energy use

Energy consumption matters most when the purifier will run for long periods. Treat ENERGY STAR as an energy-efficiency signal, not proof of filtration effectiveness. EPA states that the ENERGY STAR label does not certify air-cleaning performance.

## Safety / disqualifiers

Reject or investigate further when any of these apply:

- intentional ozone generation;
- unclear room-size claims with no comparable performance metric;
- no practical source for replacement filters;
- a maintenance design that makes correct filter replacement unlikely;
- safety alerts, recalls, or credible evidence of electrical or mechanical defects;
- marketing claims that imply filtration replaces source control, ventilation, mold remediation, or other necessary controls.

## Usability and friction

Evaluate features according to whether they help the purifier run consistently:

- usable low-noise fan settings;
- controls that remain understandable without an app;
- filter-change access and clear replacement indicators;
- easy cleaning of prefilters and air intakes;
- display dimming or light-off behavior for bedroom use;
- stable placement and practical footprint;
- automatic modes only when their sensor behavior is documented well enough to trust.

A feature that increases complexity without improving operation should not outweigh stronger filtration, lower noise, or easier maintenance.

## Reliability and durability

Laboratory performance does not establish long-term reliability. When credible owner-survey or long-term test data exist, use them to look for recurring fan, sensor, control, motor, or power-supply failures.

Prefer sources that explain how reliability data were collected. Consumer Reports and Which? provide useful methodological examples because they combine controlled testing with owner or reliability surveys instead of treating isolated reviews as population-level evidence.

## Maintenance and consumables

Record:

- filter part number;
- recommended replacement interval;
- current filter cost;
- whether third-party filters change fit or performance claims;
- prefilter cleaning requirements;
- whether filter availability has remained stable;
- any reset or app dependency after replacement.

Maintenance burden should be treated as part of product performance because a purifier cannot deliver its rated performance with a neglected or unavailable filter.

## Total cost of ownership

Compare more than purchase price. Include:

- initial device price;
- expected annual filter cost;
- electricity use at the setting likely to be used;
- expected useful life when credible data exist;
- warranty length and support quality.

A cheaper purifier can cost more over time if it uses expensive proprietary filters or consumes substantially more energy.

## Privacy / connectivity

Wi-Fi and app control are optional conveniences, not core air-cleaning requirements. For connected models, consider whether basic operation works without an account or cloud service and whether the manufacturer documents data collection and software support.

Do not prefer a connected model over an otherwise better non-connected model unless the connected feature solves a real use-case need.

## How to compare candidates

Use this order rather than a universal weighted score:

1. Remove candidates with safety or maintenance disqualifiers.
2. Check room-size suitability using credible CADR data.
3. Compare particle-filtration performance at the operating noise level that matches the use case.
4. Check filter availability and annual consumable cost.
5. Compare reliability evidence, energy use, controls, footprint, and other secondary factors.
6. Treat price as a trade-off against the criteria above, not as evidence of performance.

For a bedroom, the practical winner may be the purifier that delivers enough clean air quietly rather than the model with the highest maximum-speed CADR.

## Current market snapshot

No product snapshot is maintained in this first pilot. The guide is intended to remain useful across model generations. A future snapshot should state its date and region and evaluate named products against the criteria above.
